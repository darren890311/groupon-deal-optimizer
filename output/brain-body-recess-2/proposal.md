# Optimization Proposal

## Executive summary
This deal has a credibility problem and a content problem. The title promises "Up to 51% Off" but the largest actual discount across the four price options is 16.7% — that gap is almost certainly suppressing conversion (only "3 bought") and is a refund/compliance risk. Compounding that, the highlights section is functionally empty (just "Need To Know Info" and "Where To Redeem"), fine print is blank, and the page buries the merchant's single best asset: founder Parnisia Thomas is a board-certified massage therapist with 20+ years of experience working with professional athletes (license MA00016843). Highest-impact moves: (1) reconcile the headline discount with reality, (2) rewrite highlights to surface the experience differentiators reviewers rave about ("every little detail was so well thought out", private suite, foot soak, sound healing), and (3) add real fine print + a credentials-led trust block. Don't lead with deeper discounting — at $160 for a 60-min couples massage this deal is already priced below Seattle direct-book rates ($240–$300 at peers like Spa Reimagined and Ananya).

## Competitive positioning
Direct-book at Brain & Body Recess's own site is $389 for a 60-min Couples Recess Massage; Seattle peers like The Spa Reimagined ($300 for 60-min couples) and Ananya Spa ($355+ packages) sit in the same premium tier. Against that, the Groupon $160 / 60-min couples and $241.50 / 75-min botanicals prices are genuinely strong — the problem is the page doesn't frame the savings against the merchant's own direct rate, it frames it against an inflated "original price" that produces a believability-destroying "Up to 51% Off" claim that the price grid doesn't support. The right competitive frame is "$389 direct → $160 on Groupon" using the merchant's published rate as anchor, paired with the premium-studio signals (board-certified founder, 20+ yrs, private suite, botanical/sound-healing rituals) that justify the tier vs. cheaper $85–$150 couples options on Yelp like Wellness Station Auburn or Serenity Balance.

## Recommendations
### 1. [pricing_display] (priority 1)
**Current:** Title claims 'Up to 51% Off'; actual discounts shown are 10.0%, 11.1%, 16.7%, and 11.4%.

**Proposed:** Either (a) reset original prices against the merchant's published direct rates (e.g., 60-min Couples Recess is $389 direct on brainandbodyrecess.com → $160 Groupon = 59% off, which supports a legitimate 'Up to 59% Off' headline), or (b) drop the 'Up to 51% Off' claim and use 'Up to 17% Off' truthfully. Strongly prefer (a): re-anchor every SKU to the merchant's site price.

**Why:** The single largest credibility killer on the page. The headline promises a half-off deal; the price grid shows ~10–17% off. Customers who scroll bounce, and those who buy are likely refund risks. Meanwhile the merchant's own site lists the 60-min couples massage at $389 — that's the legitimate anchor and it actually justifies a much bigger discount claim than the page currently makes.

**Evidence:** Audit prices: $127.80→$115.02 (10%), $180→$160 (11.1%), $289.80→$241.50 (16.7%), $439.20→$389.20 (11.4%). Merchant site (brainandbodyrecess.com/massage): 'Couples Recess Massage 60 … 1 hr. 389 US dollars. $389.'

**Expected impact:** Resolves the headline-vs-grid contradiction that is almost certainly the #1 reason this deal sits at '3 bought'; restores trust and unlocks a defensible 50%+ savings claim.

**Why:** Highlights is the most-scanned module on a Groupon page and right now it contains zero benefits — just two navigation labels. Every differentiator (private suite, foot soak, sound healing, founder credentials) is buried inside a wall-of-text description. Reviewers specifically call out the bespoke care: "we were asked to fill a form asking about specific body needs / issues" and "every little detail was so well thought out."

**Evidence:** Audit highlights: ['Need To Know Info', 'Where To Redeem']. Description text contains all the actual inclusions but they're not surfaced. Yelp/MapQuest review: 'Experience was EVERYTHING! When we went for our appointment, we were asked to fill a form asking about specific body needs / issues.'

**Expected impact:** Largest single conversion lever — turns the highlights module from dead space into the page's primary sell.

### 3. [fine_print] (priority 1)
**Current:** "" (empty)

**Proposed:** Add: 'Promotional value expires 120 days after purchase. Amount paid never expires. Limit 1 per person, may buy 1 additional as gift. Limit 1 per visit. Valid for option purchased only — not combinable with other offers. Appointment required; 24-hr cancellation policy. New customers only / Returning customers welcome [merchant to confirm]. Merchant is solely responsible to purchasers for the care and quality of the advertised goods and services. Licensed massage therapist on staff (WA #MA00016843).'

**Why:** Empty fine print is one of the top drivers of post-purchase refund tickets and chargebacks in the massage category — customers buy not knowing the cancellation window, eligibility, or whether tip is included. Filling it is cheap insurance.

**Evidence:** Audit: fine_print = "". Category norm on Groupon couples-massage listings is a populated terms block including expiration, per-person limits, appointment policy, and new/returning eligibility.

**Expected impact:** Materially reduces post-purchase refund/dispute volume and clarifies eligibility before checkout.

### 4. [title] (priority 2)
**Current:** 60-Min Signature Recess™, 75-Min Botanicals Recess™, Or 90-Min Luxury Recess™ Couples Massage (Up to 51% Off)

**Proposed:** Couples Massage with Private Suite & Botanical Foot Soak — 60, 75, or 90 Min at Brain & Body Recess (Up to 59% Off)

**Why:** Current title leads with three trademarked product names a shopper has never heard of and pushes the words 'Couples Massage' to position 9. Search demand and category browsing is on 'couples massage' — that should be in the first three words. Adding 'Private Suite & Botanical Foot Soak' differentiates from the $85–$150 budget couples options and matches what reviewers describe as the experience.

**Evidence:** Category page at groupon.com/local/seattle/couples-massage shows competing titles lead with format/duration ('50-Min Signature Back Massage w/ Hot Stone Treatment for 1 or a Couple'); trademark-first naming is non-standard. Description confirms 'Botanical foot soak · Hot stone enhancements · Private suite · Complimentary beverages' as Signature inclusions.

**Expected impact:** Lifts CTR from category and search by aligning the title with how shoppers actually browse.

### 7. [pricing_display] (priority 2)
**Current:** Four SKUs listed including a single-person '60 Minute Swedish Massage for One' ($127.80→$115.02, 10% off) on a deal whose title is exclusively about couples massage.

**Proposed:** Either remove the solo Swedish SKU from this deal (it doesn't belong on a couples-massage page and its 10% discount drags down perceived value), or split it into a separate solo deal. If kept, retitle to '60-Min Solo Swedish Massage' and re-anchor the original price.

**Why:** The solo Swedish option is off-brand for a couples deal, has the smallest discount of the four, and is the SKU pulled into the SEO meta_title ('From $127.80'), which both undersells the deal and confuses the offer. Removing it tightens the offer and lifts the floor price shown in search.

**Evidence:** Audit: title is 100% couples-focused; first SKU is solo. Audit seo.meta_title: 'Brain & Body Recess - From $127.80 - Seattle | Groupon' — this 'from' price is the solo SKU.

**Expected impact:** Cleaner offer, higher 'from' price in search snippets, removes the weakest discount from the price grid.

**Current:** urgency_signals: ['limited time']
