# Optimization Proposal

## Executive summary
This page is leaving money on the table because the pricing math is upside-down: per Booksy, Action Sports Massage's own direct rates are $105 (60 min) and $150 (90 min), but Groupon's listed 'original price' is $87.99/$120.99 — meaning the actual savings vs. booking direct are ~24% and ~27%, not the '9%' the page math implies or the '19%' the title claims. Fix the anchor and the discount story instantly looks 2-3x stronger without changing the deal price. Second, the title and highlights are generic ('Custom Massage') while every five-star review names a specific value prop — Tucker's deep-tissue / sports-injury skill, education on stretching, and the Lake Union / China Harbor view — none of which appear in the highlights. Third, the page underuses powerful trust assets: 4.7★ from 436 reviews and 'THREE decades' serving Lake Union (per the merchant's Yelp bio) are not surfaced as badges. Reframing pricing, rewriting the title around 'sports / deep tissue', and surfacing the longevity badge are the three changes most likely to move conversion this week.

## Competitive positioning
Compared to Seattle alternatives, this deal is genuinely well-priced — Seattle Massage MDB charges $179/60 min and $255/90 min; Elements North Seattle $139/$199; Urban Calm Spa $85/$125; Wellbeing Reimagined ~$135/60 min. The merchant's own direct rate is $105/$150 (Booksy). At $80.07/$110.10, this Groupon undercuts virtually every comparable provider AND undercuts the merchant's own walk-in price. But the page is positioning it as a thin 9-19% discount off an inflated anchor, which signals 'mediocre deal' to a Groupon shopper trained to expect 40-50% off. The page should reposition as 'Lake Union's 30-year sports-massage specialist at below-direct pricing' — competing on therapist expertise + tenure, not on a discount percentage that looks weak by category norms.

**Why:** Booksy lists Action Sports Massage's published rates as $105 (60 min) and $150 (90 min). The current $87.99/$120.99 anchors are LOWER than what the merchant charges direct, which makes the Groupon 'discount' look like 9% when it's actually 24-27% off the real walk-in price. Groupon massage category benchmarks routinely show 40-70% off; a 9% discount badge underperforms category norms and signals weak value. Fixing the anchor is the single highest-leverage change on this page.

**Evidence:** Booksy: 'Action Sports Massage & Kickboxing ; 60 min massage. $105.00 ... 90 min massage · $150.00 $127.50'. Title currently reads 'Up to 19% Off' but the page math shows only 9%.

**Expected impact:** Materially lifts perceived value and click-to-buy on category and search pages by turning a 9% badge into a 24-27% badge — without changing the deal price.
